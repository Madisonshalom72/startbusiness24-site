Delaware or Wyoming?

  • The investor-ready default — the common choice when outside investors are part of the plan.
  • A long-established system for corporations — generations of company law and a dedicated business court.
  • Built around stock — the C-Corp structure investors recognize is the home turf here.
  • The low-friction favorite — a popular choice for many owner-run companies.
  • No state corporate income tax — for most companies, in general terms.
  • Simple yearly upkeep — an annual report keeps the company in good standing.

Questions, Answered Plainly

General, educational answers — not legal or tax advice. Your situation is yours; a CPA or attorney in your state has the final word on decisions like these.

Either can be chosen in the questionnaire. In general terms: an LLC is the flexible default for many owner-run businesses, taxed as a pass-through by default; a C-Corp is a separate taxpayer with stock, and is the common structure when outside investors are part of the plan. Which fits depends on how you will operate, own, and get paid — this page explains the difference educationally; it is not advice for your specific case.

The questionnaire itself takes minutes to complete. After you file, approval timing is set by your state — the state stamps the filing on its own schedule, not this page's.

The standard next steps: get your EIN, open a business bank account, set your taxes calendar, keep your corporate records, and stay current on yearly compliance. The After You Form section above walks the list, and the free training courses cover each step in plain English.

Delaware or Wyoming are the states shown in the formation flow. Delaware is the common choice for companies expecting outside investment; Wyoming is a popular, low-friction choice for many other businesses. Where you actually operate can create filing duties in that state too — check your situation before you choose.
